Coding The Enigma of Deep-Sea Bioluminescence
The hadal zone, the deepest trenches of the ocean, remains one of the most mysterious frontiers on Earth, characterized by crushing pressure and absolute darkness. In this alien environment, life has evolved a spectacular array of bioluminescent adaptations to survive. Organisms like the anglerfish and various species of jellyfish utilize chemical reactions to produce light, serving as lures for prey, signals for potential mates, or even "burglar alarms" to startle predators. These shimmering displays create a rhythmic, neon-lit ecosystem in the abyss, proving that even in the absence of sunlight, biological innovation finds a way to illuminate the void.
